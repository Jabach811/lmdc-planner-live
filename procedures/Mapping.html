Fund
mapping.
Same wire, more bookkeeping.

A wire arrives, the same as cash — but each prior fund maps to a receiving fund per the signed TOA. The mapping applies twice: once on wire day to the incoming assets, once post-wire to participant accounts.

§ 03 · Twin apply

The mapping fires twice.

This is the rule that catches new analysts: the mapping doesn't apply once. It applies on the wire and again on participant accounts after reversal.

First apply

Wire day · incoming

Fund mapping is applied to the wire as it lands. Same-day workflow follows the Day of Wire pattern.

This places the assets correctly at the plan level so reconciliation can begin.

⌖ runs against the wire breakdown
Second apply

Post-wire · participant

After mapping reversal, the same fund mapping is applied to participant accounts so each individual lands in the receiving fund.

Skipping this leaves balances at the plan level only.

⌖ runs against the final file
§ 03b · Flow

Wire arrives. Mapping fires twice.

The plan-level apply is the bookkeeping pass. The participant-level apply is the one that actually moves assets into the right fund per person — skip it and balances strand at the plan level.

Key rule The mapping reversal happens post-wire — fund mapping applies twice: once on wire day to incoming assets, once post-wire to participant accounts.
Developer-mode tip Workflow failures that look like a clean file are often hidden metadata issues. Always validate in Informatica dev mode before the final run — inspect what the workflow actually sees, not just what the file looks like.
Do not use until QA approves The mapping document is approval-gated. Running an unapproved doc into the live workflow doesn't fail loudly — it just produces wrong receiving balances on real participant accounts.
